## Disease Background

### Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD)

Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD) is an inherited metabolic disorder that affects the body's ability to break down the branched-chain amino acids leucine, isoleucine, and valine. The disorder results from reduced activity of the branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKD) complex, causing these amino acids and their related metabolites to accumulate in the body.

MSUD can affect the nervous system and may cause symptoms such as poor feeding, vomiting, lethargy, abnormal muscle tone, neurological problems, seizures, and developmental difficulties. In severe cases, untreated MSUD can progress to encephalopathy and coma. The severity of the condition can vary depending on the amount of remaining BCKD enzyme activity.

One of the genes associated with MSUD is **BCKDHA**, which encodes the E1 alpha subunit of the BCKD complex. This subunit is involved in the normal breakdown of branched-chain amino acids. Mutations in the BCKDHA gene can alter the structure or function of the E1 alpha subunit and may reduce the activity of the BCKD complex. This disruption in amino-acid metabolism contributes to the biochemical and neurological effects associated with MSUD.

MSUD follows an **autosomal recessive inheritance pattern**, meaning that affected individuals generally inherit disease-causing variants in both copies of an associated gene. Studying BCKDHA mutations helps demonstrate how changes in DNA can alter a protein and eventually contribute to a disease phenotype.

## Project Summary

This project investigates the relationship between a **BCKDHA gene mutation**, its effect on the encoded protein, and the molecular basis of **Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD)**. The normal BCKDHA coding sequence from the NCBI RefSeq transcript **NM_000709.4** was used as the wild-type reference.

The documented mutation **c.929C>G (p.Thr310Arg)** was reproduced in a copy of the wild-type coding sequence. The mutant sequence was translated and compared with the wild-type protein. The analysis showed that the mutation changes the codon from **ACA to AGA**, resulting in a **threonine-to-arginine substitution at amino-acid position 310 (T310R)**. The protein length remained 445 amino acids because the mutation did not cause a frameshift or premature stop codon.

An additional artificial mutation, **c.303G>A**, was also introduced into the BCKDHA coding sequence. This changed the codon from **AAG to AAA**, but both codons encode lysine. Therefore, the artificial mutation was predicted to be **synonymous** and did not change the predicted amino-acid sequence.

Overall, the project demonstrates how different types of nucleotide changes can produce different effects at the protein level. The documented BCKDHA mutation produces a missense change that may affect protein stability and BCKD complex function, while the artificial mutation does not alter the predicted protein sequence.
